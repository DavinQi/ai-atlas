Post-training: From "Continues Text" to "Follows Instructions and Reasons"

Companion to diagram #7 · Chapter 1 "How LLMs Are Trained"

Overview

A pretrained base model only continues text: ask it a question and it may simply write more questions instead of answering. Post-training uses orders of magnitude less data than pretraining to turn the base model into a usable assistant. The mainstream recipe has evolved through three generations:

  1. SFT: supervised fine-tuning on high-quality instruction–response demonstrations teaches conversational format and basic instruction following.
  2. Preference optimization: comparisons of response quality by humans (or AI) push the model toward preferred behavior. The classic approach is RLHF (reward model + PPO); the simpler DPO came later.
  3. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR): on tasks where answers can be checked automatically, such as math and code, large-scale RL with rule-based rewards elicits long-chain reasoning. DeepSeek-R1 is the landmark example.

Core Mechanisms

1. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT). The loss is the same next-token cross-entropy as pretraining, computed only on the response tokens, using human-written or curated demonstrations. LIMA fine-tuned 65B LLaMA on just 1,000 curated examples; in a controlled human study its responses were equivalent or preferred to GPT-4 in 43% of cases. The authors proposed the "superficial alignment hypothesis": almost all knowledge is learned in pretraining, and SFT mainly teaches which format and style to answer in.

2. RLHF. Learning from preferences was introduced for deep RL by Christiano et al. in 2017, validated on summarization, and systematized by InstructGPT into three steps:

  • Step 1: SFT. About 13k training prompts with labeler-written demonstrations.
  • Step 2: Reward model (RM). About 33k prompts; labelers rank several outputs for the same prompt. A Bradley–Terry model turns rankings into pairwise comparisons:

LRM​=−E(x,yw​,yl​)​[logσ(rϕ​(x,yw​)−rϕ​(x,yl​))]

where yw​ is the preferred response and yl​ the worse one.

  • Step 3: RL with PPO. About 31k prompts; maximize reward while a KL penalty keeps the policy from drifting too far from the SFT model:

πθ​max​ Ex,y∼πθ​​[rϕ​(x,y)]−βDKL​[πθ​(y∣x)∥πref​(y∣x)]

PPO itself is a policy-gradient algorithm by Schulman et al. that limits each update by clipping the probability ratio. InstructGPT observed that RLHF degraded performance on some public NLP benchmarks — an "alignment tax" — and mitigated it by mixing pretraining gradients into PPO (PPO-ptx).

3. DPO. Rafailov et al. showed that the KL-constrained objective above has a closed-form optimal solution in which the reward can be expressed through the policy itself, so both the reward model and the RL loop can be dropped and preferences optimized directly:

LDPO​=−E[logσ(βlogπref​(yw​∣x)πθ​(yw​∣x)​−βlogπref​(yl​∣x)πθ​(yl​∣x)​)]

The paper reports that DPO beats PPO-based RLHF at controlling sentiment and matches or improves response quality on summarization and single-turn dialogue, while being much simpler to implement and train and requiring no sampling from the model during fine-tuning.

4. RLVR and GRPO. When an answer can be checked by a program — compare a math answer, run code against tests — the reward no longer depends on a reward model that can be gamed. Tulu 3 named this approach "Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards" (RLVR) and combined it with SFT and DPO into a complete open post-training recipe.

GRPO, introduced in DeepSeekMath, is a PPO variant: sample a group of responses per prompt and normalize each response's reward by the group mean and standard deviation to get its advantage. This removes the value network (critic), which is as large as the policy, and reduces memory use.

5. The DeepSeek-R1 pipeline:

  • R1-Zero: RL applied directly to the DeepSeek-V3 base model, with no SFT. Rewards are entirely rule-based (accuracy + format). The paper explicitly avoids neural reward models for reasoning tasks because it observed them to be susceptible to reward hacking in large-scale RL. During training, average pass@1 on AIME 2024 rose from 15.6% to 77.9%, reaching 86.7% with self-consistency decoding, and behaviors such as self-reflection and verification emerged (the paper's "aha moment").
  • R1-Zero's problems: poor readability and language mixing.
  • R1's fix: SFT on thousands of cold-start long chain-of-thought examples → reasoning RL (with a language-consistency reward) → rejection sampling to produce new data, combined with non-reasoning data for another round of SFT → a second RL stage for helpfulness and harmlessness.

Engineering Practice and Numbers

  • Data scale: InstructGPT's three stages used about 13k, 33k and 31k prompts — negligible next to trillions of pretraining tokens, but with very high quality requirements.
  • An industrial recipe: Llama 3's post-training iterates SFT, rejection sampling (using a reward model to pick the best of several candidates) and DPO over multiple rounds, rather than more complex RL algorithms like PPO.
  • Open and reproducible: Tulu 3 releases the full data, code and recipe (SFT → DPO → RLVR) on top of Llama 3.1 — the best starting point for building your own post-training pipeline.
  • Choosing a method:
    • Only need to adjust format or style: SFT on a small, high-quality dataset (optionally with parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA).
    • Need preference alignment: DPO is the simplest and most stable.
    • Need higher reasoning accuracy and have auto-gradable tasks: RLVR / GRPO.

Common Misconceptions

  • "RLHF is real RL and can improve indefinitely." Karpathy calls RLHF "just barely RL": the reward model only approximates human preference, unlike Go's objective win/loss signal, and with enough optimization the model finds outputs that fool it. This is why R1 chose rule-based rewards.
  • "SFT can inject lots of new knowledge." LIMA concludes the opposite: SFT mainly teaches format.
  • "DPO has fully replaced RL." DPO uses offline preference data. The big gains in reasoning come mainly from RL with online sampling (e.g. GRPO + verifiable rewards).
  • "Alignment is free." InstructGPT documented an alignment tax.
