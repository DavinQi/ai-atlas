Data and Tokenization: Text Becomes Tokens, Tokens Become Vectors

Companion to diagram #4 · Chapter 1 "How LLMs Are Trained"

Overview

A model never "sees" characters. Raw text is first cleaned and deduplicated, then a tokenizer splits it into discrete tokens, each mapped to an integer id, and an embedding matrix turns each id into a vector that enters the Transformer. Data quality sets the model's ceiling; the tokenizer determines how much text a given compute budget can "read" and whether costs are fair across languages.

Core Mechanisms

1. The data pipeline. Take FineWeb: it builds a 15T-token dataset from 96 Common Crawl snapshots and systematically ablates its deduplication and filtering choices; its 1.3T-token educational subset, FineWeb-Edu, gives markedly better results on knowledge- and reasoning-heavy benchmarks such as MMLU and ARC. Typical steps: text extraction → language identification → heuristic and model-based quality filtering → deduplication (e.g. MinHash) → PII and harmful-content filtering.

2. Subword tokenization (BPE). Sennrich et al. brought byte-pair encoding (BPE) to neural machine translation: start from characters (or bytes) and repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair into a new symbol until the vocabulary reaches its target size. Common words become a single token, rare words split into subwords, and there are no out-of-vocabulary words. SentencePiece treats raw text (spaces included) directly as a sequence, without language-specific pre-tokenization — a good fit for languages like Chinese that don't separate words with spaces.

Karpathy stresses that the tokenizer is a completely separate stage of the LLM pipeline, with its own training set and training algorithm (BPE), and after training it exposes two functions: encode() and decode().

3. Embeddings. With vocabulary size V and hidden size d, the embedding matrix is E∈RV×d, and token id i maps to row i of E. Position is injected by a positional encoding; modern models mostly use RoPE, which rotates queries and keys by position so that attention scores depend only on relative position.

Key Papers and Milestones

  • 2015 BPE subwords
  • 2018 SentencePiece
  • 2021 RoPE
  • 2024 FineWeb / FineWeb-Edu: a public 15T-token dataset with its full construction pipeline and ablations.
  • 2024 Llama 3: a 128K vocabulary.

Engineering Practice and Numbers

The Llama 3 report gives direct evidence on vocabulary design:

  • 128K vocabulary: 100K tokens from tiktoken plus 28K additional non-English tokens.
  • Compared with the Llama 2 tokenizer, compression on English samples improved from 3.17 to 3.94 characters per token, which the authors note lets the model "read" more text for the same training compute.
  • The 28K non-English tokens improved compression and downstream performance in those languages with no impact on English tokenization.
  • The RoPE base frequency was raised to 500,000 to support longer contexts.

Engineering implications:

  • APIs bill by token. The same Chinese text can produce very different token counts on different models; measure with the actual tokenizer before estimating cost.
  • A larger vocabulary means more parameters in the embedding and output layers, but shorter sequences — cheaper attention and a smaller KV cache.
  • Deduplication affects both quality and evaluation validity: if test items leak into training data, scores are inflated.

Common Misconceptions

  • "Models understand text character by character, or word by word." They see subword ids. Many failures on digits, spelling and character counting stem from tokenization granularity.
  • "More data is always better." FineWeb-Edu shows a smaller, filtered subset can clearly beat a larger unfiltered one.
  • "You can swap tokenizers freely." The embedding matrix is tied to the vocabulary; changing tokenizers usually means retraining or extensive continued training.
