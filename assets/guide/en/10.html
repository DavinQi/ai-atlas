Training Cost: Each Step Up in Scale Is a Jump in Order of Magnitude

Companion to diagram #10 · Chapter 1 "How LLMs Are Trained"

Overview

The cost table in the diagram is the speaker's order-of-magnitude estimate. Rather than repeat those numbers, this article gives a method you can recompute yourself, anchored on real figures from public reports. The core conclusions: pretraining cost is roughly proportional to parameters × training tokens; post-training is usually a small share; and the total investment in a frontier model goes far beyond the GPU bill of a single training run.

Core Mechanisms

1. From FLOPs to GPU hours. Training compute is roughly C≈6ND (N parameters, D tokens), so:

GPU hours≈peak FLOPS per GPU×MFU×36006ND​

MFU (model FLOPs utilization) is the fraction of peak compute actually used. Llama 3 405B achieved 38–43% BF16 MFU on 8K–16K H100s. NVIDIA lists the H100 SXM at 1,979 BF16 TFLOPS, but that figure is with sparsity; dense compute is about half, roughly 989 TFLOPS.

2. Worked example: a 70B model on 2.2T tokens (the token count in the diagram's 70B row):

  • C=6×7×1010×2.2×1012≈9.2×1023 FLOPs
  • At 989 TFLOPS and 40% MFU: about 9.2×1023/(3.96×1014×3600)≈650,000 H100 hours
  • At $2 per GPU hour (the same rental assumption DeepSeek-V3 uses for H800s): about $1.3M

This is pure compute for one final run. The diagram's estimate also includes data and other costs and gives ranges, so the two use different scopes. Real prices vary widely by hardware, contract and region; the example only illustrates the method.

3. MoE counts active parameters. DeepSeek-V3 has 671B total and 37B activated parameters per token, and needs only 180K H800 hours per trillion pretraining tokens. Plugging in active parameters: 6×3.7×1010×1012=2.2×1023 FLOPs, the same order of magnitude as the report.

Key Papers and Milestones

  • 2020 Kaplan: C≈6ND

  • 2024 DeepSeek-V3: a full public GPU-hour bill
  • 2024 Epoch AI: systematic estimates of frontier training costs

Engineering Practice and Numbers

DeepSeek-V3's official bill:

Stage H800 GPU hours
Pretraining (14.8T tokens) 2,664K
Context extension 119K
Post-training 5K
Total 2,788K

At $2 per GPU hour, the total is $5.576M. The report states explicitly that this covers only the official training run, excluding prior research and ablation experiments on architectures, algorithms and data. Karpathy commented at the time that this amounted to 2,048 GPUs for 2 months, about $6M, whereas this level of capability was supposed to require clusters closer to 16K GPUs.

Easily overlooked costs:

  • Failures and effective training time: during a 54-day period, Llama 3 405B pretraining saw 466 job interruptions, 419 of them unexpected, with about 78% attributed to confirmed or suspected hardware issues; the team still achieved over 90% effective training time.
  • R&D and staff: Epoch AI estimates that for frontier models such as GPT-4 and Gemini, the two largest expenses are AI accelerator chips and staff, each tens of millions of dollars; server components are 15–22%, cluster interconnect 9–13%, and energy only 2–6%.
  • Growth trend: since 2016, the amortized cost of training the most compute-intensive models has grown about 2.4× per year (90% CI: 2.0–2.9×); if the trend continues, the largest runs will exceed $1B by 2027.
  • Post-training share: DeepSeek-V3's post-training was only about 0.2% of total GPU hours (5K / 2,788K). But the share of RL for reasoning is rising and varies widely across models, so don't generalize.

Common Misconceptions

  • "DeepSeek-V3 built a frontier model for just $5.6M." That is the rental-price equivalent of the official run, excluding research, ablations, staff and hardware purchases.
  • "Cost depends only on parameter count." At the same size, doubling tokens doubles compute.
  • "MoE training cost scales with total parameters." Compute follows active parameters, but memory, communication and load balancing add engineering cost.
