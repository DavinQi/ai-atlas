Pretraining and Scaling Laws: Predict the Next Token, Trade Scale for Lower Loss

Companion to diagram #6 · Chapter 1 "How LLMs Are Trained"

Overview

Pretraining is the most expensive step in the pipeline: minimizing next-token cross-entropy over trillions of tokens. Its most important empirical regularity is the scaling law: test loss falls smoothly, as a power law, in parameter count N, data size D and compute C. You can therefore fit curves on small models, predict large-model performance, and allocate budget accordingly.

Core Mechanisms

1. Estimating compute. Kaplan et al. give the standard approximation: about 6N non-embedding FLOPs per training token (forward 2N, backward 4N), so total compute is:

C≈6ND

2. Kaplan power laws (2020):

L(N)=(NNc​​)αN​,αN​≈0.076;L(D)=(DDc​​)αD​,αD​≈0.095

The trends span more than seven orders of magnitude, and architectural details such as width and depth matter little within a wide range. They concluded that compute-optimal training means very large models trained on relatively modest data and stopped well before convergence.

3. The Chinchilla correction (2022). DeepMind trained over 400 models (70M to over 16B parameters, 5B–500B tokens) and fit:

L(N,D)=E+N0.34A​+D0.28B​,E=1.69, A=406.4, B=410.7

The conclusion: for compute-optimal training, parameters and training tokens should scale equally — every doubling of model size should come with a doubling of data. Chinchilla (70B parameters, 1.4T tokens), trained on the same compute as Gopher (280B), outperformed it across a wide range of downstream tasks, reaching 67.5% average on MMLU — more than 7 points above Gopher. The ratio is often summarized as "about 20 tokens per parameter" (1.4T ÷ 70B = 20).

Key Papers and Milestones

  • 2020 Kaplan scaling laws
  • 2020 GPT-3 (175B)
  • 2021 Gopher (280B)
  • 2022 Chinchilla: showed that the large models of the time were significantly undertrained.
  • 2024 Llama 3: sized the 405B model using scaling laws fit on its own data.

Engineering Practice and Numbers

  • Llama 3 405B: 3.8×1025 FLOPs, 15.6T tokens. The report says this size is approximately compute-optimal for that budget according to scaling laws on its data. Sanity check with C≈6ND: 6×4.05×1011×1.56×1013≈3.8×1025 — consistent.
  • Why "over-train" today? Chinchilla optimizes training compute only. If a model will serve huge inference volumes, training a smaller model on more tokens lowers per-request cost. The Chinchilla paper itself notes that smaller models make fine-tuning and inference cheaper.
  • Parallel training: hundred-billion-scale models combine data, tensor and pipeline parallelism (Megatron-LM), and use ZeRO to partition optimizer states, gradients and parameters to save memory.

Common Misconceptions

  • "Emergent abilities appear abruptly." Wei et al. documented abilities absent in small models but present in large ones; Schaeffer et al. argued that many such jumps come from discontinuous metrics, and that with continuous metrics the curves become smooth and predictable.
  • "Scaling laws guarantee downstream ability." The power laws describe cross-entropy loss; the link between loss and task scores must be evaluated separately.
  • "Kaplan and Chinchilla contradict each other." The Chinchilla paper attributes part of the difference to Kaplan's use of a fixed number of training tokens and learning-rate schedule for all models; in practice, what matters most is refitting on your own data.
