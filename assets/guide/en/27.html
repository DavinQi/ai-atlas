Metrics: Measure Delivery Outcomes, Not the Share of AI-Written Code

Companion to diagram #27 · Chapter 4 "AI Coding Today"

Overview

When teams roll out AI development, the first instinct is to watch two numbers: how many people use AI, and how much code AI writes. Alibaba's handbook notes these are useful during cold start, but a measurement system built only around them goes astray: code written by AI isn't necessarily committed, committed code isn't necessarily released successfully, and released code doesn't necessarily deliver requirement value. The real question measurement must answer is not "how much AI was used" but "what AI changed".

Core Mechanisms

1. A three-layer system (per Alibaba's handbook):

  • L3 Value delivery: requirement delivery cycle, change cycle, release frequency, AI vs non-AI delivery comparison. Verifies whether AI makes the business faster and more stable.
  • L2 Engineering quality: AI defect rate, rollbacks and rework, self-fixed defects, risk events. Ensures L1 growth doesn't come at the cost of quality.
  • L1 AI efficiency: coverage, sessions, tokens, AI lines, Skills, MCP, context assets. Drives real adoption and pinpoints tooling and context-engineering problems.

The layers can't be read alone: L1 alone encourages "AI for AI's sake"; L2 alone becomes a quality audit; L3 alone can't explain where change came from. L1 proves AI entered the process, L2 proves quality risk is contained, L3 proves business results improved — together they form a complete evidence chain.

2. Alignment with DORA. L3 and L2 closely match the industry-standard DORA software delivery metrics:

  • Throughput: change lead time (commit to production deployment), deployment frequency, failed deployment recovery time.
  • Instability: change failure rate, deployment rework rate (the share of unplanned deployments caused by production incidents).

3. Attribution. The handbook describes lightweight hooks adapting various AI tools, normalizing into standard events, and attributing along Session → Commit → Change → Workitem.

Key Papers and Milestones

  • DORA metrics: the most widely used software delivery performance measures
  • 2023 Copilot randomized controlled experiment: 55.8% faster on a well-bounded experimental task
  • 2025 METR randomized controlled trial: experienced developers on familiar mature projects took 19% longer when allowed AI, yet afterwards still estimated they had been 20% faster

Engineering Practice and Numbers

  • Single metrics create perverse incentives: the handbook notes install rate counts users who installed but show no effective development activity; sessions mistake ad hoc Q&A for productivity; AI code share incentivizes useless generation.
  • No individual rankings: once you rank, teams optimize the metric rather than the real collaboration process.
  • Measure context assets too: whether Skills, MCP, Specs, README / runbooks, templates and checklists are reused, updated and verified.
  • Perception is unreliable: in METR's study developers expected a 24% speedup, estimated 20% afterwards, and were measured 19% slower. Use objective data.

Common Misconceptions

  • "The higher the AI code share, the better." It measures usage, not value, and can incentivize useless generation.
  • "A shorter delivery cycle proves AI works." It may also come from process changes or simpler requirements; attribution needs process evidence.
  • "Surveys are enough." Perception and measurement can point in opposite directions.
